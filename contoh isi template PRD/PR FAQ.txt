# PR FAQ

Desc:
Amazon-style press release from the book Working Backwards. Written in the format of a Press Release and FAQs after the successful launch of a product.

Instructions: Write in the voice of a press release announcing a successfully launched product. The tone should be confident and newsworthy — written for a major publication, not an internal memo. Quotes should feel authentic and specific to the product. FAQs should address real concerns and tough questions, not softballs. Internal FAQs should surface genuine strategic, operational, and financial considerations.

Template                                          8 Sections 

## Heading
Name the product in a way the target customer will immediately understand.

### Format
- **Product name**: Clear, memorable, customer-facing name
- **Tagline**: One sentence underneath that captures the core value proposition

The heading should work as a real headline — if a journalist saw it, they'd immediately grasp what's being announced.

## Subheading
One sentence that describes who the customer is and what benefit they gain.

### Format
[Product name] helps [target customer] [achieve specific benefit].

This should be benefit-focused (what the customer gets), not feature-focused (what the product does).

## Summary Paragraph
The press release lead paragraph — sets the scene for the announcement.

### Format
**[City], [Media Outlet] — [Proposed Launch Date]** — [Company name] today announced [product name], a [brief description] that [key benefit for customers].

### Guidelines
- Write as if this is a real wire service press release
- Lead with the most newsworthy aspect
- Keep to 3-4 sentences maximum
- Include a concrete detail that makes it tangible

## Problem Paragraph
Describe the customer problem this product solves — written entirely from the customer's perspective.

### Guidelines
- Start with the customer, not the company: 'Today, [customers] struggle with...'
- Use specific, vivid details about the pain (time wasted, money lost, frustration)
- Quantify the problem where possible ('spend 4 hours per week manually...'
- Make the reader feel the problem before they hear the solution
- Do NOT mention your product in this paragraph

## Solution Paragraph(s)
Describe how the product solves the problem — clearly and simply.

### Guidelines
- Lead with the outcome: 'With [product], [customers] can now [benefit]'
- Explain how it works in plain language — no jargon
- Highlight 2-3 key capabilities that directly address the problems above
- For complex products, use multiple short paragraphs rather than one long one
- Include a specific example or scenario that makes the solution concrete

## Quotes & Getting Started
### Company Spokesperson Quote
A quote from a leader at your company. It should:
- Express the vision and why this matters
- Sound like a real person talking, not marketing copy
- Add context the press release paragraphs didn't cover

### Customer Quote
A hypothetical quote from a target customer. It should:
- Describe the specific benefit they experienced
- Reference a real use case or workflow
- Sound authentic and enthusiastic without being over-the-top

### Getting Started
One paragraph on how easy it is to start using the product. Include a call-to-action and link.

## External FAQs
Questions a customer, journalist, or analyst would ask after reading the press release.

### Format
**Q: [Question]**
A: [Answer]

### Example Questions
- How much does it cost? / What's the pricing model?
- How is this different from [competitor]?
- When is it available? / How do I get access?
- What data do you collect and how is it secured?
- Does it integrate with [common tool]?

Answers should be direct and specific — no deflecting. If something is TBD, say so.

## Internal FAQs
Questions leadership, the board, or other teams would ask internally.

### Format
**Q: [Question]**
A: [Answer]

### Example Questions
- Why is this the right problem to solve now?
- What's the estimated revenue impact or market size?
- How much will this cost to build and maintain?
- What are the biggest risks? What could go wrong?
- How does this fit into our broader product strategy?
- What do we need from other teams to make this successful?

Be honest and thorough — internal FAQs should surface the hard questions.
