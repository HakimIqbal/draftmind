# User Personas

Desc:
Detailed profiles representing different user segments to create empathy and better design for user needs.

Instructions: Create vivid, research-grounded personas that feel like real people — not marketing stereotypes. Include specific behavioral details that directly inform product decisions. Quotes should sound natural and reveal genuine frustrations or aspirations. Each persona should have distinct needs that lead to different product implications.

Template                                          8 Sections 

## Persona Name
A memorable, descriptive name that the team can reference in discussions.

### Format
- **Name**: A real-sounding first name + a descriptive qualifier
- **Example**: 'Startup Sarah' / 'Enterprise Eli' / 'Freelancer Fran'
- **Photo suggestion**: Describe the type of stock photo that represents this persona

The name should instantly evoke who this person is — make it stick in the team's memory.

## Demographics
Factual information that sets the context for this persona's needs and behaviors.

### Key Details
- **Age range**: e.g., 28-35
- **Job title & seniority**: e.g., Senior Product Manager
- **Company size**: Startup (<50) / Mid-market (50-500) / Enterprise (500+)
- **Industry**: e.g., SaaS, E-commerce, Healthcare
- **Location**: Urban/suburban/remote, region
- **Technical skill**: Non-technical / Technical-adjacent / Developer

Only include demographics that actually influence product decisions — skip what doesn't matter.

## Goals
What this persona is trying to achieve — both with your product and in their broader role.

### Product Goals
What they want to accomplish using your product:
- Goal 1: [Specific outcome they want]
- Goal 2: [Another specific outcome]

### Career/Role Goals
Broader objectives that your product helps with:
- [Underlying motivation: look competent, save time, get promoted, reduce stress]

### Jobs to Be Done
The 'job' they are 'hiring' your product to do:
- **When I** [situation], **I want to** [motivation], **so I can** [expected outcome]

## Pain Points
Specific frustrations this persona experiences — grounded in research, not assumptions.

### Current Frustrations
For each pain point:
- **Pain**: What specifically frustrates them
- **Frequency**: How often they encounter this
- **Severity**: Annoying / Painful / Blocking
- **Current workaround**: What they do today to cope

### Quotes from Research
Direct quotes that capture the frustration in their own words:
- *"I spend half my Monday just pulling together the weekly numbers from three different tools."*
- *"I know there's a better way but I don't have time to figure it out."*

## User Journey
How this persona typically interacts with your product category.

### Discovery
- How they first learn about products like yours
- Where they go for information (Google, communities, peers, Twitter)

### Evaluation
- What criteria matter most when comparing options
- Who else is involved in the decision (manager, team, IT)

### Adoption
- First actions they take when trying a new tool
- What determines whether they stick with it or churn

### Ongoing Usage
- Typical frequency and depth of use
- What triggers them to engage (notification, workflow, habit)

## Behavioral Traits
Key characteristics that influence how this persona uses products.

### Technology Behavior
- **Tool adoption**: Early adopter / Pragmatist / Late majority
- **Learning style**: Explores independently / Reads docs / Needs hand-holding
- **Platform preference**: Mobile-first / Desktop / Both equally

### Work Style
- **Decision making**: Data-driven / Intuition-based / Consensus-seeking
- **Communication**: Slack/async / Meetings / Email
- **Pace**: Moves fast and iterates / Plans carefully before acting

### Personality
2-3 key adjectives that define how they approach problems (e.g., detail-oriented, pragmatic, skeptical, collaborative).

## Quotes from User Research
Direct quotes from interviews, surveys, or support conversations that bring this persona to life.

### Format
*"[Quote that reveals a key insight about their needs or frustrations]"*
— Context: [Where/when this was said]

### Guidelines
- Include 3-5 quotes that each reveal something different
- Mix positive quotes (what they love) with negative (what frustrates them)
- Quotes should feel real — messy, specific, and human
- If using hypothetical quotes, base them closely on actual research themes

## Product Implications
What this persona means for product decisions — the actionable takeaway.

### Design Implications
- UI complexity level this persona can handle
- Key workflows to optimize for this persona
- Onboarding approach that matches their learning style

### Feature Priority
- Features this persona values most
- Features this persona doesn't care about
- Gaps in the current product for this persona

### Messaging
- Value proposition that resonates with this persona
- Language and tone that works (technical, simple, aspirational)
- What competitors this persona is comparing you to
