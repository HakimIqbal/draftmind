# OKRs

Desc:
Generate a list of objectives and key results based on your product and business goals

Instructions: Write objectives that are ambitious but achievable within the stated timeframe. Key results must be specific and measurable with concrete numbers — avoid vague outcomes like "improve" or "increase." Each initiative should clearly connect to its parent key result. Use the user's actual context for timelines, not generic placeholders. Risks should be realistic and paired with actionable mitigations.

Template                                          8 Sections 

## Objective
Clearly state the main goal you want to achieve. This should be ambitious but achievable.

### Guidelines
- **Qualitative, not quantitative** — objectives describe the destination, key results measure progress
- **Inspiring but grounded** — should motivate the team and feel attainable
- **Time-bound** — specify the quarter or timeframe
- **Aligned** — should connect to company-level strategy

### Example
'Become the go-to solution for small teams managing customer feedback' (Q2 2026)

## Key Results
Measurable outcomes that indicate progress toward the objective. Aim for 3-5 per objective.

### Format
KR[#]: [Verb] [metric] from [baseline] to [target] by [date]

### Guidelines
- Each KR must be **specific and measurable** with a concrete number
- Include the **current baseline** — you can't measure progress without a starting point
- Mix **leading indicators** (actions) and **lagging indicators** (outcomes)
- Set a **stretch target** (70% achievement = good) not a sandbagged one

### Example
- KR1: Increase weekly active users from 2,000 to 5,000
- KR2: Achieve NPS score of 50+ (currently 32)
- KR3: Reduce average onboarding time from 15 min to 5 min

## Initiatives
High-level projects or workstreams that will drive the key results. 1-3 initiatives per key result.

### Format
For each initiative:
- **Initiative**: What you will do
- **Drives KR**: Which key result(s) this supports
- **Owner**: Who is responsible
- **Effort estimate**: T-shirt size (S/M/L/XL)

### Guidelines
- Initiatives are the 'how' — they connect the measurable KRs to actual work
- Each should be concrete enough to staff and schedule
- If an initiative doesn't clearly drive a KR, question whether it belongs

## Alignment
How these OKRs connect to the bigger picture.

### Company Alignment
- Which company-level objective(s) these support
- How success here contributes to overall business goals

### Cross-Team Dependencies
- Other teams whose OKRs overlap or depend on yours
- Shared key results or handoff points
- Potential conflicts to resolve early

### Strategic Context
Why these objectives matter NOW — what market conditions, customer feedback, or business priorities make this the right focus.

## Key Stakeholders
Who is responsible for each key result and initiative.

### Format
| Role | Person | Responsibility |
|------|--------|----------------|
| OKR Owner | [Name] | Overall accountability for the objective |
| KR1 Lead | [Name] | Drives KR1, reports progress weekly |
| KR2 Lead | [Name] | Drives KR2, reports progress weekly |

### Escalation Path
Who makes the call when trade-offs are needed between competing KRs or when a KR is off-track.

## Timeline & Checkpoints
When results are expected and how progress is tracked.

### Milestones
- **Week 2**: [Early signal or setup milestone]
- **Month 1**: [First meaningful progress checkpoint]
- **Mid-quarter**: [Halfway check — are we on track?]
- **End of quarter**: [Final measurement and scoring]

### Cadence
- **Weekly**: Quick status update (on track / at risk / off track)
- **Bi-weekly or monthly**: Deeper review with stakeholders
- **End of quarter**: Full scoring and retrospective

## Risks and Mitigations
What could prevent you from hitting these OKRs.

### Format
For each risk:
- **Risk**: What could go wrong
- **Likelihood**: Low / Medium / High
- **Impact**: Which KR(s) would be affected
- **Mitigation**: What you'll do to prevent or reduce this risk
- **Contingency**: What you'll do if it happens anyway

### Common OKR Risks
- Dependency on another team that has different priorities
- Metric measurement is harder than expected (no baseline, tooling gaps)
- Scope creep on initiatives — doing too much, achieving nothing
- External factors (market shift, competitor launch, regulation)

## Review & Scoring
How progress will be measured and what happens after the cycle.

### Scoring Method
- **0.0 – 0.3**: No meaningful progress
- **0.4 – 0.6**: Some progress but fell short
- **0.7 – 0.8**: Strong result (target for stretch goals)
- **0.9 – 1.0**: Fully achieved (goal may have been too easy)

### Review Process
- When is the end-of-cycle review?
- What format (written self-assessment, team meeting, leadership review)?
- How do learnings feed into next cycle's OKRs?

### Retrospective Questions
- What drove our best results?
- What would we do differently?
- Which OKRs should carry over, evolve, or be dropped?
